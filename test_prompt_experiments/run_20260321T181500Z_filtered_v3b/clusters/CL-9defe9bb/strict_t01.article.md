# France's Stance on Strait of Hormuz Ship Escorts

## Summary
French President Emmanuel Macron has stated that France will not participate in operations to open or liberate the Strait of Hormuz until the situation calms down. He emphasized that France is not a party to the conflict.

## Body
The French president, Emmanuel Macron, has made it clear that France will not engage in any operations aimed at opening or liberating the Strait of Hormuz until the situation becomes less volatile. This stance reflects France's commitment to neutrality in the ongoing tensions surrounding the strategic waterway.

Macron's statement comes in response to the U.S. President's criticism of European allies for their reluctance to join in efforts to ensure the free passage of oil tankers through the Strait of Hormuz. The French president reiterated that France will only consider participating in such operations once hostilities have ceased.

Additionally, the impact of the ongoing conflict on the fishing industry in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) has been highlighted. Fishermen in the region have faced significant challenges, with fishing boats being confined to ports along the coastline near the Strait of Hormuz. This has led to a standstill in the fishing sector in Dubai and a rise in fish market prices.

While France is willing to assist in escorting ships through the Strait of Hormuz, it insists on doing so only after the situation has stabilized. This cautious approach underscores the complex geopolitical dynamics surrounding the region.
