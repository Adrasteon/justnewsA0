# France's Stance on Strait of Hormuz Ship Escorts

## Summary
French President Emmanuel Macron has stated that France will not participate in operations to open or liberate the Strait of Hormuz until the situation calms down. He emphasized that France is not a party to the conflict.

## Body
The French president, Emmanuel Macron, has made it clear that France will not engage in any operations aimed at opening or liberating the Strait of Hormuz until the situation becomes more stable. This stance reflects France's position of neutrality in the ongoing tensions surrounding the strategic waterway. Macron's statement underscores France's commitment to avoiding direct involvement in the conflict, emphasizing that they are not a party to the dispute.

Macron's comments align with previous statements where he expressed reluctance to join any immediate military actions in the region. However, he did indicate a willingness to assist in escorting ships through the strait once the situation has calmed. This cautious approach reflects France's desire to avoid being drawn into what they perceive as a conflict initiated by external pressures, particularly from Israel and its supporters in the United States.

The situation in the Strait of Hormuz remains tense, with concerns over freedom of navigation for oil tankers. While the United States has called for international assistance to address these issues, France's response highlights the complexities and varying stances among nations regarding intervention in the region.
