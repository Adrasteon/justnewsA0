# Global Response to Trump's Demand for Strait of Hormuz Control

## Summary
President Donald Trump has requested that NATO allies and other countries send warships to keep the Strait of Hormuz open, but many countries have rejected this demand. Several countries including Australia, Japan, Poland, Sweden, and Spain have explicitly stated they will not send military ships. The German Defence Minister, Boris Pistorius, and Italy’s Foreign Minister, Antonio Tajani, have also rejected the request.

## Body
The ongoing conflict between the United States and Israel against Iran has intensified, leading to significant casualties primarily in Iran. As of the latest reports, more than 1,500 people have been confirmed dead, with over 1,400 casualties in Iran alone. This war has entered its eighteenth day, with strikes continuing to escalate.

In response to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz by Iran, President Donald Trump has made multiple requests to allied nations to assist in reopening the strategic waterway. The Strait of Hormuz is crucial as it facilitates approximately 20% of the world's oil supply. However, many countries have declined to participate in military operations aimed at reopening the strait.

Several countries, including Australia, Japan, Poland, Spain, and Sweden, have explicitly stated their refusal to send military ships to the region. Additionally, Germany and Greece have ruled out military involvement in the Strait of Hormuz. The German Defence Minister, Boris Pistorius, and Italy’s Foreign Minister, Antonio Tajani, have both rejected the request, emphasizing the importance of diplomatic solutions over military intervention.

President Trump has expressed frustration over the lack of support from his allies, particularly those within NATO. He has criticized countries like Germany and Italy for their reluctance to join the effort, despite previous military support from the United States. However, the President has not specified which countries he claims have shown interest in assisting with the operation.

The French President, Emmanuel Macron, has also distanced his country from any direct military involvement in the Strait of Hormuz. He stated that France will not participate in operations to open or liberate the strait under the current circumstances but is willing to contribute to an escort system once the situation stabilizes.

These responses highlight the complex geopolitical dynamics surrounding the conflict and the reluctance of many nations to engage in direct military action in the region.
