# Costs of US War with Iran

## Summary
The US war with Iran has incurred significant costs, with estimates ranging widely. Initial Pentagon estimates suggest a cost of around $11.3 billion for the first week, while experts and government officials estimate daily costs between $1 billion and $2 billion. Long-term costs could reach trillions of dollars over decades.

## Body
The United States' engagement in the war with Iran has seen substantial financial expenditures. According to Pentagon officials, the cost of operations during the first week was approximately $11.3 billion. However, experts and government insiders provide a different perspective, estimating that the war incurs between $1 billion and $2 billion daily. These figures highlight the vast discrepancy between official statements and expert assessments.

One government official, speaking anonymously, expressed concern that the war's costs could escalate to a quarter trillion dollars within months. This figure pales in comparison to the potential long-term expenses, which could amount to trillions of dollars over the coming decades. Such costs would likely burden taxpayers for generations to come.

Democratic lawmakers argue that the true cost is significantly higher due to the exclusion of pre-conflict military buildups and ongoing expenses. They anticipate a supplemental budget request reaching at least $50 billion, in addition to the existing $1.5 trillion War Department budget for 2027. Critics contend that these funds could be better utilized for domestic programs such as healthcare and education.

Despite these concerns, the Trump administration has not provided comprehensive cost estimates. Undersecretary of War for Policy, Elbridge Colby, admitted during a recent appearance before the House Armed Services Committee that he could not provide a definitive cost figure. This lack of transparency fuels skepticism about the true extent of the war's financial impact.
