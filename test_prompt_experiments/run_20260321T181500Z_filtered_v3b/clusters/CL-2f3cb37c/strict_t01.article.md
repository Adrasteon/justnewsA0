# Costs of US War with Iran

## Summary
The US war with Iran is reported to have cost at least $11.3 billion in just one week, according to Pentagon estimates. However, experts and government officials suggest the actual costs could be significantly higher, ranging from $1 billion to $2 billion per day. These figures do not account for pre-conflict military buildups and long-term expenses.

## Body
The Trump administration's estimates of the cost of the U.S. war with Iran are significantly lower than those suggested by experts and government insiders. According to Pentagon officials, the U.S. spent approximately $11.3 billion in just one week of the conflict. However, experts and government officials briefed on Operation Epic Fury claim that the actual costs are much higher, ranging from $1 billion to $2 billion per day. This discrepancy highlights the potential underestimation of the war's financial impact by official sources.

One government official expressed concern that the costs could rise to a quarter trillion dollars or more over the coming months. Furthermore, long-term expenses could amount to trillions of dollars over the next few decades. This raises significant concerns about the sustainability of such expenditures and the potential burden on future generations.

Democratic lawmakers argue that the Pentagon's estimate is incomplete, as it does not include the costs associated with the buildup of military assets, weapons, and personnel prior to the conflict. They anticipate that the total cost of the war could reach at least $50 billion, in addition to the $1.5 trillion War Department budget request for 2027. The lack of transparency regarding the true cost of the war has led to criticism from both fiscal hawks and Democrats, who argue that the funds could be better utilized for domestic programs such as healthcare and education.

Despite these concerns, the administration has yet to provide a comprehensive figure for the war's cost. Under Secretary of War for Policy, Elbridge Colby, admitted during a recent appearance before the House Armed Services Committee that he could not provide a specific cost estimate at that time. This lack of clarity further fuels skepticism about the accuracy and completeness of the official cost estimates.
