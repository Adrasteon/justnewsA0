# Zelenskyy Meets Starmer Amid Rising Tensions

## Summary
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met with UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer in London to discuss maintaining sanctions pressure on Russia and signing a new defense pact. The meeting comes amid concerns about global attention shifting towards the Middle East conflict.

## Body
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met with UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer in London to discuss maintaining sanctions pressure on Russia and signing a new defense pact. The meeting took place at 10 Downing Street and included NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte. The discussions centered around peacemaking efforts in Ukraine and the need to maintain sanctions pressure on Russia. Zelenskyy sharply criticized the United States' decision to temporarily ease some sanctions on Russian oil exports, arguing that this move could embolden Moscow and provide financial support to sustain its military campaign in Ukraine. Starmer emphasized the importance of keeping the focus on Ukraine amidst the unfolding US-Israeli war on Iran, which threatens to divert international attention away from Russia's attacks on Ukraine. The leaders signed a new defense pact aimed at boosting global defensive capability against the proliferation of low-cost, high-tech military hardware. The deal combines Ukraine's expertise in building high-tech, battle-tested drone interceptors with the UK's industrial base to manufacture and supply drones and innovative capabilities. Starmer highlighted the strategic importance of emerging military technologies, stating that drones, electronic warfare, and rapid battlefield innovation are now central to national and economic security, further magnified by the conflict in the Middle East. Meanwhile, Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan offered to host the next round of negotiations between Moscow and Kyiv, emphasizing the risks posed by the prolonged war for regional countries and the international order. This offer comes as US-brokered talks between delegations from Russia and Ukraine have stalled due to the escalation of the war in the Middle East.
