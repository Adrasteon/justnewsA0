## Body
Joe Kent, the director of the National Counterterrorism Center, resigned from his position on Tuesday, citing his opposition to the ongoing war in Iran. Kent, a former Army Special Forces officer with extensive combat experience, expressed his belief that Iran posed no imminent threat to the United States. He attributed the initiation of the war to pressure from Israel and its influential lobby in the U.S. Kent’s resignation highlights his disagreement with President Donald Trump’s handling of the conflict, stating that the war was unnecessary and detrimental to American interests. Kent’s departure marks a significant break from his previous support for Trump’s policies, especially those related to national security. Kent’s military background includes over two decades of service, including deployments across Iraq and Yemen. He also served as a paramilitary officer with the CIA and as a counterterrorism advisor to Trump’s 2020 reelection campaign. Kent’s political career was marked by associations with far-right figures, including Nick Fuentes, a white nationalist, and the Proud Boys, a far-right organization. Despite these associations, Kent maintained his loyalty to Trump until his resignation. In response to Kent’s resignation, Trump criticized Kent’s stance on security, calling him “weak on security.” Trump emphasized that Iran was indeed a threat to the United States and other countries. Kent’s resignation letter, posted on X, detailed his concerns about the war and urged Trump to reconsider the current course of action. The resignation raises questions about the internal dynamics within the Trump administration and the broader implications for U.S. foreign policy.
