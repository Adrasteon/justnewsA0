## Body
Joe Kent, the director of the National Counterterrorism Center, resigned from his position on Tuesday, citing his opposition to the ongoing war in Iran. Kent, a former Army Special Forces officer with extensive combat experience, expressed his belief that Iran posed no imminent threat to the United States and that the war was initiated due to pressure from Israel and its influential lobby in the U.S. Kent emphasized his support for President Donald Trump’s initial foreign policy stance, which he felt avoided unnecessary entanglements in the Middle East. However, he criticized the current administration for being misled into believing that Iran posed an immediate threat, leading to military action. Kent’s resignation highlights internal disagreements within the Trump administration regarding the justification for military strikes in Iran. In response, President Trump dismissed Kent’s concerns, stating that he always thought Kent was “weak on security” and that Iran was indeed a significant threat to the United States. Kent’s background includes a history of ties to far-right political figures and conspiracy theories, which raised concerns among Democrats during his confirmation process. Despite these concerns, Kent was confirmed to his position in July 2025 by a narrow margin in the Senate. Kent’s wife, Shannon, a Navy cryptologic technician, was killed in action in Syria in 2019, which Kent referenced in his resignation letter as a reason for his opposition to the war in Iran.
